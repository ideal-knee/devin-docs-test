# The Case of the Borrowed Hatchet

The trouble at Camp Wobegone Pines started, as most trouble does, with a
casserole.

Ranger Deb had left it on the picnic table outside Cabin Four at 6:40 p.m. By
7:05 p.m. the casserole was gone, the dish was upside down in a huckleberry
bush, and someone had taken the camp hatchet off the woodpile and driven it
neatly into the trunk of a Douglas fir at a height of exactly six feet four
inches.

"That's a message," said Ranger Deb.

"That's a hatchet," said Milo, age eleven, who had recently declared himself
the camp's only licensed detective on the grounds that nobody had checked.

Milo took notes. Milo's notebook was a spiral one from the gift shop with a
picture of a moose on it, and he had written on the cover, in permanent marker,
*EVIDENCE — DO NOT READ (MILO)*. This was the first mistake of the
investigation, because it guaranteed that everyone read it.

## Suspects

**Chef Bruno**, who had made the casserole and therefore had motive, means, and
the strongest opinions about tuna of anyone in three counties. Bruno said he had
been in the kitchen the entire time. The kitchen had a window facing the picnic
table. Milo wrote down *SUSPICIOUS WINDOW*.

**The Hollingsworth twins**, ages nine, who could not be ruled out because they
could not be located, and who were later found on the roof of the boathouse
where they had been for two days, living, they claimed, "off the land." The land
in question was a bag of marshmallows.

**Gary**, a black bear of local renown with a documented history of casserole
crimes. Gary had an alibi, in that Gary had been photographed eleven miles away
at a gas station in Pinedale, looking directly into the security camera like he
knew.

**Ranger Deb herself**, whom Milo included in the notebook out of a sense of
professional thoroughness, and then crossed out, and then un-crossed out, and
then drew a small question mark next to, which Deb saw, and which affected their
relationship for the remainder of the summer.

## The Investigation

Milo measured the hatchet's height with a length of paracord and the confidence
of a man who does not know what a tape measure is. Six feet four. Nobody at camp
was six feet four. Chef Bruno was five feet six in his clogs. Ranger Deb was
five feet nine. The tallest person on the property was a canoe.

"So the culprit," Milo announced at the evening campfire, "is a giant."

"Or," said Priya, age twelve, who was not a licensed detective and resented the
whole arrangement, "someone stood on something."

Milo wrote down *PRIYA — HOSTILE WITNESS*.

The next clue arrived at 9:15 p.m., when the Hollingsworth twins reported seeing
"a big shape" moving through the trees behind the latrines. Milo went to
investigate with a flashlight and returned four minutes later at considerable
speed, having discovered that the big shape was the latrines.

By midnight he had assembled a theory involving Gary, a stepladder, and an
accomplice. By 1 a.m. he had abandoned it, because Gary cannot drive to Pinedale
*and* operate a stepladder, and Milo had standards.

## The Solution

The break came from the huckleberry bush.

Milo, retrieving the casserole dish for the fourth time, noticed that the bush
sat directly beneath the low branch of a lodgepole pine, and that the branch had
been scuffed clean of bark in a strip about the width of a boot.

Not a giant. A climber.

The hatchet had not been driven into the fir at six feet four inches by a person
standing on the ground. It had been driven in by a person standing on a stump —
a stump that was, Milo confirmed by pacing it out, no longer where it had been
on Tuesday, because Chef Bruno had rolled it behind the woodshed to use as a
table for cooling pies.

Which meant the hatchet had gone into the tree *before* Tuesday.

Which meant the hatchet had nothing to do with the casserole at all.

"I put that there in June," said Ranger Deb, when asked. "I was trying to hang a
bird feeder and I got frustrated."

"And the casserole?"

Deb was quiet for a moment. "Gary has a brother," she said.

## Epilogue

The brother's name is Curtis. Curtis is smaller than Gary, faster than Gary, and
has never been photographed at a gas station in Pinedale, because Curtis is
careful. Curtis has taken four casseroles, one lasagna, and a birthday cake with
the words HAPPY RETIREMENT DOUG still legible in the wreckage.

Camp Wobegone Pines now stores all food in a locked steel bear box, which Curtis
visits nightly and regards with the patience of a professional waiting out a
temporary setback.

Milo closed the case and wrote his conclusion in the moose notebook:

> *SOLVED. Two crimes, not one. Also the latrines are fine.*

Priya read it, because of the permanent marker.
